Getting Started in the Ocean (Local Guide)

Living in Hawaiʻi, the ocean is part of everyday life — whether you're bodyboarding at the beach, fishing off the shore, or just jumping in for a swim. But before you get in the water, it’s important to know how to stay safe and respect the ocean conditions we deal with here.

Step 1: Read the Ocean Before You Go In

Locals always check the ocean first. Even if it looks calm, conditions can change fast. Look at the waves, wind, and current before deciding to go in.

  • Check surf conditions before heading out
  • Watch for strong currents or rip tides
  • Ask locals if you’re unsure about conditions
  • If it looks too rough, don’t force it

Step 2: Choose Something Beginner-Friendly

If you’re just starting out, don’t jump straight into heavy surf or deep water. Start with something simple and build your comfort in the ocean.

  • Bodyboarding: Great for learning waves close to shore
  • Fishing: Relaxed shoreline activity, common in Hawaiʻi
  • Shallow snorkeling: Good way to explore safely

Step 3: Use the Right Gear

You don’t need fancy equipment to start, but having the basics helps keep you safe and comfortable in local waters.

  • Rash guard or swim shirt for sun protection
  • Reef-safe sunscreen
  • Proper board or gear for your activity
  • Flotation aid if you’re still learning

Local Ocean Advice

One thing you learn quickly living near the ocean is respect. Don’t just jump in anywhere — watch what the ocean is doing, stay aware, and never assume conditions are safe just because it looks nice from shore.

When in doubt, stay close to shore and learn from people who know the area well.
